## What is in it

| Ticket | Change | Record |
|---|---|---|
| 001 | Usage: `prompt_tokens` is the whole input, prompt-cache reads and writes included (Anthropic, ACP); Gemini batch folds thinking into `completion_tokens`; AG-UI drops its per-provider cache correction. **Breaking** | D-126, MIGRATION §11 |
| 002 | Agent: a forced response (final tool result, client-tool hand-off) ends the turn; queued input waits for the next turn | D-127 |

| 006 | ACP: agent-client-protocol 1.0 parity — chunks grouped by `message_id` (`TurnResult.Text` is the last message), absolute `session/new` paths, uri-only image / `resource` / `resource_link` blocks; the `env_var` auth method and its API are removed. **Breaking** | D-131, MIGRATION §11 |
| 007 | Test: the AG-UI retention expiry test drives a fake clock instead of a 40 ms window | known-issues |
| 008 | AG-UI: held turns are released outside the registry lock, and `release` no longer waits (`aclose` does), as in Python. Fixes a hang of the whole AG-UI endpoint on supersede, `MaxHeld` overflow or expiry; a POST after expiry answered in ~3 ms instead of never | acceptance 008 |

**Breaking changes** (both in `docs/MIGRATION.md` §11, hence a minor bump):
1. On a cached turn `prompt_tokens`/`total_tokens` grow by the cache counts — `TokenMonitor` thresholds,
   `UsageReport` sums, metric counters and `otelx` span attributes move with them. Example (ticket 001):
   prompt 100 → 1050, total 120 → 1070. The recipe for the old figure is in §11.
2. `acp`: `AuthKindEnvVar`, `NewEnvVarAuthMethod`, `AuthEnvVar` and `AuthMethod.Vars` are gone (removed from
   the protocol in 1.0). `TurnResult.Text` is now the turn's last message, not every message glued together;
   `TurnResult.Messages` carries all of them.

## Speed measurement
Benchmark: `benchmarks/agobench` (plain_chat, tool_roundtrip, streaming, concurrent), `v0.10.0` vs the
candidate `4d1e0b6`, same machine, alternating, 6 pairs of `-warmup=5 -runs=30`.

**Wall-clock timing does not resolve a difference.** The VPS was running another project's work (load
average ~7 on 6 cores), and the paired runs spread from −33 % to +199 % on tool_roundtrip (−58 % to +224 %
across all scenarios). The medians of the six pairs were plain_chat +2.3 %, tool_roundtrip +11.7 %,
streaming −6.2 %, concurrent −20.2 %; with this spread they are noise, not a measurement. `bench-compare`
flagged tool_roundtrip over the 10 % threshold on these numbers.

**Allocation evidence (load-independent).** Per iteration, `runtime.MemStats` around 2 000 / 1 000 / 1 000 /
100 iterations after a warm-up, two runs per version, via a build overlay (the repository was not changed):

| scenario | mallocs/iter v0.10.0 → candidate | bytes/iter v0.10.0 → candidate |
|---|---|---|
| plain_chat | 239.0 → 239.0 | 19 862 → 19 864 |
| tool_roundtrip | 544.1 → 544.1 | 67 862 → 67 867 |
| streaming | 577.0 → 577.0 | 40 185 → 40 185 |
| concurrent | 8 403.9 → 8 403.2 | 683 100 → 683 004 |

The only change on the benchmarked path is the order of the checks in `agent.turnBody`'s tool loop
(`ResponseForce` is tested inside the loop, ticket 002); it adds no work.
**Owner decision (2026-11-20): release on the allocation evidence (precedent D-125).** A repeat on a quiet
host was not waited for. Raw data: thread storage `release/bench/`.
